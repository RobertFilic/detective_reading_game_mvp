# Lessons: puzzle designer

Read before every task. Add up to 3 lessons after every task: the rule, then — evidence (what happened, where). A lesson confirmed twice moves up to **Rules**; a lesson proved wrong is deleted. Keep this file under ~60 lines. Playtest evidence beats review opinion.

## Rules
- Every puzzle's hint ladder ends in the answer, and a puzzle that depends on an earlier case gives that answer away in its last hint, so skipping a case never blocks a child. — the lock in Bip 5 (h2 says 3-1-4).

- **Cipher cap: 20 letters and 9 different.** Count the different letters before giving a cipher a slot; a proof or an alibi of 5+ words never fits (a words geslo or a torn note does). — Sneg 3 (»Ponoči ni zvonilo.« = 8), Igrače 3 (the plan's »Rdeč trak ima samo Nuša.« = 14, so the cipher carries »Paket ni cel.« = 9).

## Lessons
- **Playtest favourite: the number lock 🔐.** The producer's children »really like the numbers and the locker« (2026-11-05). Keep a number lock in every new season, with its code found by reading in an earlier step or case (as 3-1-4 in Bip 4 → 5), and vary how the digits are found (a count, a clock, a list). — producer, 2026-11-05. Applied in Muzej 4: 4 wheels, a countdown read across four calls. Promote to Rules once a playtest confirms the children found the code.
- No auto-check while the child turns the lock's wheels or places timeline cards; check only on »Odpri« / »Preveri«. Otherwise trying everything is faster than thinking. — Bip 5, Bip 6.
- In a timeline, near-synonyms (»Potem«, »Nato«) make an order ambiguous unless the content decides it. Use that on purpose for exactly one reasoning step (bring the wire before wrapping it), never by accident. — Bip 6.
- After a wrong timeline check, keep the right cards (green) and send back the wrong ones: the child sees progress without being told the answer. — Bip 6.
- Word search grids need a check for rude words by chance in every row and column, both directions; the checker does it (`BLOCK` in tools/cases.mjs). — Bip 4.
- Design for the smallest screen first: the word grid, the lock and the timeline all needed layout changes to fit iPhone SE and a landscape phone without scrolling to the main button. Put that in every spec. — Bip 4–6.
- Slovenian word order is free, so a torn note can be right in more than one order. `also` is **not built**: the torn step checks exactly one order. Split the strips so that the capital, the full stop and one meaning point (a strip ending in a preposition, »spal v«) leave a single grammatical order, try every permutation, and have the Slovenian editor confirm it. — Galeb 4 design; Muzej 2 (»Fosil je | ves večer | spal v | kleti.«).
- Check what each step can hand over before matching types to slots: `cipher`, `torn` and `words.geslo` give a pocket sentence; `lock` and `order` give none (the lock opens the next step, the timeline has a `done` line). A slot just before 🧭 or ❗ needs a sentence-giving puzzle; the lock only fits where a scene or a questioning follows it. — Muzej plan: the lock could only go in case 4.
- A code or clue planted in a letter is not on screen in time: letters arrive on the next visit, so a child playing two cases in one sitting never sees it. Plant codes in calls, scenes or answers. A running line (Breda's countdown) read in every call makes a good code, and the wheel count can tell the child how many numbers to find. — Muzej 4 (5-4-3-2), design.
- When a season's skill is a step of its own (UGOVOR), count it as the skill, not a puzzle. Give the case that teaches it no other puzzle. Let the final combine the season's new puzzles at one notch harder, so no brand-new mechanic lands in the final. — Galeb plan; check it at the playtest (hint level on case 1's UGOVOR, the time the final takes).
- Count the words of every draft line with the checker's rule before handing it over (emoji and »…« count as words). Two lines in the Galeb drafts were 9 words, one of them from the pitch. — Galeb, 2026-11-01.
- A lock's `h2` must contain the code's digits **once**: the checker strips every non-digit and compares. A score code can't be stated as »3 : 1 in 2 : 0. Šifra je 3-1-2-0.« (digits twice); write »Šifra je 3-1-2-0. Najprej Dinozavri, nato Ježki.« — Pokal 6 design, 2026-11-06.
- With a ⏰ `when` step, the 🟨 in solve comes only from `when.cards` (`wrongTime()` ignores `when:` on a sentence then), so **every wrong-time sentence of that case must be a card**. Count them before choosing the card number. — Pokal 4: the pitch's 3 cards left the culprit's own wrong-time line out of play, so it became 4.
- Give a new puzzle knob an **additive** data format (an extra item the old renderer ignores, like the riddle as `find[i][2]`), so the fallback when the build slips is the same data, and only the hint that mentions the new thing (»črtice«) changes. — Pokal 4 spec.
- Before specifying help for a step, check whether the child can ask for it without being wrong first: today hints in solve and the puzzles come only after wrong tries, so a child who does not know what to do is stuck unless they guess. A free, always-available hint tap is the base of any »help that fades«. — WARMUP_AND_HELP spec, 2026-11-12.
- Companion lines in Slovenian must be gender-neutral when four different companions share them (🐶 m, 🐱 🦉 🦊 f): imperative, present tense, dual (»Preberiva«), never first-person past. — WARMUP_AND_HELP spec.
- A warm-up must teach the loop real cases use (call, scene, cross off, proof); the old 🎓 practice taught a classic case (pick culprit, tap proof) that no season uses. Check what the tutorial teaches against the step cases before reusing it. — WARMUP_AND_HELP spec.
- Skrivnostne sobe: do not hand-tune maps; give a design script the map (rooms, objects, missing thing's room) and let it search placements and one sentence per person, then hill-climb the sentences on "number of solutions + free cells" until unique, and only then test "every sentence needed" and the child's step count. Random sampling alone yielded ~1 in 100000 on 5×5 and nothing on 6×6. — rooms 13–22, 2026-11-15.
- In 🟢 puzzles every »on X« object must exist once on the map (two klopi make a hidden row/column step and fail the 0-step rule), and the culprit's room needs a seat or the culprit gets the no-clue sentence. The label of a room needs a free cell, so a room with one free cell left for the culprit has no place for its name. — rooms 13–22.
- The stop sentence of the rules sheet takes »je/so« from the first blocking object in `objects`, so list a singular one first (smuči is plural). — rooms 13–22.
- Easiest 5×5 recipe for Skrivnostne sobe (0 row/column steps): four »on object« sentences (each object once on the map) plus one person with no clue, who is the leftover last row and column. Put the extra person's object on a row and column that leave the free person's cell in the right room. Moving an unused object to a corner is enough; no search script was needed. — rooms 13, 15, 19, 21, redrawn 5×5 on 2026-11-15; the checker's `lahko` limit (0 steps) needed no change.
- Skrivnostne sobe difficulty is not row/column steps alone: in the 2026-11-15 playtest (one 7-year-old) 7 (1 step, no »ni ob«) and 13 (0 steps, all »na«) were easy, but 8 (1 step + »ni ob«) was the hardest and needed help, harder than 9 (2 steps) and 10 (3 steps). 11 (2 steps, »ni v« + »ni na« + two room clues) was »hard, think and eliminate — excellent«, the model room. Producer: the negative sentence is not the hard part; the deduction is — eliminating cells and seeing who is the only one left for a row or column. Rate a room by how many people can't be placed straight from their own sentence (their sentence leaves several cells), not by sentence type; daily rooms need several such people, all-»na« (each placed directly) only for the very first rooms. Measure it with the »odprta polja« column the checker prints (13 → 9 easy, 7 → 25 easy, 11 → 40 hard and excellent, 8 → 41 needed help). — docs/KJE_JE_KDO.md, Playtest 2026-11-15.
- Skrivnostne sobe: a great srednje step is a **column (or row) claim**: a person whose cells all lie in one column takes that column from everyone else. In room 14 Nika (»ob zvoncu«) and Jernej (»ob zaboju«) each fit only one column, which left Mark one column — the producer rated it »relatively hard, very good«. The checker doesn't count this step (it finds another way), so name it in the room's notes when you build it on purpose. — Playtest 2026-11-16.
- Build word-search grids with a script, not by hand: place the words (one crossing if you want it), fill the free cells with the geslo in reading order, then check count = 1 per word, the blocklist, and that no cast name (≥ 4 letters) appears in a row or column (a geslo with the suspect's name in it can spell it). Words in `find` do **not** lock scene objects in the engine, whatever the old README says: they only name places. — Sneg 1 and 5 grids.
- When a season's logic says »the night's snow covers the tracks«, an absence of footprints cannot break a lie about going out at night. Pick the breaker that survives the season's own rule (the silent bell), and tell the writer in the puzzle section. — Sneg 3 (fair-play check of the pitch's »no footprints« breaker).
- A geslo's length fixes the word total: free cells = 36 − letters of the words (+ crossings). Choose the geslo sentence first (24 letters), then three words that sum to 12 (MIZA, LUČI, KLOP), and let the script place them with one word down at least. — Igrače 4.
- When the director limits lists on screen, keep puzzle screens apart from the case's lists: put an `order` before the scene (not between two lists), give outputs as sentences, and say how a words puzzle's chips differ from a clue list, with a fallback. A torn note stays unique with a lone preposition (»v« | »sobi.«); the free word order breaks any strip set without one. — Igrače 2, 4, 5.
