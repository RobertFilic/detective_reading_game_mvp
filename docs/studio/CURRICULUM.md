# The detective curriculum: how the game grows with the child

The children are 7–8 now and will be 9–10 in two years. Each season teaches **one new detective skill** and raises **one or two reading knobs**, never more. The model is Agatha Christie: Christie never made her mysteries harder by hiding clues. She made them harder by hiding them **in plain sight**, behind an obvious suspect, inside a list, in something that *didn't* happen. That is the kind of hard we want.

## The rule of growth

- **One new skill per season**, taught gently in cases 1–2, practised in 3–5, and needed for the final case.
- **Old skills come back mixed in**, so nothing is forgotten (elimination stays in every season).
- **Reading load rises slowly**: at most one knob per season from the table below, two if the playtest says the last season was easy.
- **Move up on evidence, not on the calendar.** Before a harder season, check [PLAYTEST.md](PLAYTEST.md) and the adult debrief: most cases solved with 0–1 hints, few assisted solves, the child reads on their own. If the child needed the last hint often, the next season stays at the same level and teaches its new skill more gently.
- **Earlier seasons stay as the way in** for younger siblings and new players. A season says its level on its first line.

## Reading knobs (from easy to hard)

| Knob | Season 1 (now) | Later |
|---|---|---|
| Words per sentence | ≤ 8 (level A) | 9–10 → 11–12 → ≤ 14 (level B) |
| Sentences per case | 6–8 | 9–10 → 11–12 |
| Suspects | 4 | 4–5 |
| Names vs pronouns | names only | »je rekla«, »njegov« (pronoun points to the sentence before) |
| Time words | najprej, potem, na koncu | ob osmih, preden, medtem ko, šele ko |
| Negation | rare | »ni«, »nikoli«, »nihče« (careful: a child often misses »ne«) |
| Who speaks | narration and short quotes | witnesses who contradict each other; a diary; a letter |
| Text across screens | one screen | two clues on different screens that must be combined |

## The skill ladder (one rung per season)

Rungs 1–2 are done. The order after that is a plan; the showrunner may swap neighbours for a better story.

| Rung | Skill | What the child learns | Christie's version, for children |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ✅ | **Find it and cross off** | Read every clue; cross off whoever has an alibi; the proof names the culprit | the closed circle: everyone was on the station |
| 2 ✅ | **Follow the thread** | Clues from different cases belong to one bigger story; a guess is not a proof | the recurring small oddities that only make sense at the end (Bip) |
| 3 ✅ | **The lie (UGOVOR!)** | A witness says something; another sentence shows it can't be true | the alibi that breaks: »I was in the kitchen« vs the kitchen light was off |
| 4 ✅ | **The obvious suspect is innocent** | The clue that points at someone too clearly was planted or misread | the red herring; the person everybody suspects from page one is not the one |
| 5 | **What did *not* happen** | Missing evidence is a clue: the dog didn't bark, the snow has no footprints | the dog in the night (Holmes, but Christie used it too) |
| 6 ✅ | **When, exactly?** | A true alibi for the wrong time is no alibi; order events by clock and »preden / potem« | the timetable murder, minus the murder: who could have been there at 8? |
| 7 | **The clue in a list** | One item in an ordinary list matters (a shopping list, a packing list) | Christie's favourite trick: hide the clue among boring things |
| 8 | **Why?** | Motive: who *wanted* this to happen, and why it was kind or funny | the motive revealed at the end, here always warm |
| 9 | **The least likely person** | The helper, the client, the quiet one, the one who called for help | *Roger Ackroyd*, softened: the client hid the thing, for a good reason |
| 10 | **Everyone did it** | Several small actions add up to one surprise | *Murder on the Orient Express*, as a surprise party everyone planned |
| 11 | **Explain it** | The child puts the solution in order in a denouement scene (»Zberite se vsi v salonu!«) | Poirot's final speech, built by the child from cards |

## Difficulty knobs inside one case

From [PUZZLES.md](../PUZZLES.md) and the cases so far:
- number of suspects, sentences and decoys
- where the proof sits (early / middle / late)
- the decoy's strength: a mild decoy (»Tim ima rad sir«) → a strong one (the suspect's own claim) → a planted clue
- one proof or a pair that only works together
- the puzzle's knobs (grid size, wheel count, number of timeline cards, time words given or implied)
- how early the hints come

## The fun must grow too

Harder must also mean **more fun**, never homework. Each season brings at least one new *feeling*, not only a new rule: a phone that rings at night, a ghost that turns out to be a friend, a betrayal that is really a surprise, a case the child explains to everyone. The showrunner lists that feeling in the pitch.
