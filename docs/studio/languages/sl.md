# Style guide: Slovenian (sl)

Every rule the studio follows for Slovenian text, in one place. The case writer and the Slovenian editor read it before every task. The evidence behind each rule stays in [lessons/slovenian-editor.md](../lessons/slovenian-editor.md) and [lessons/case-writer.md](../lessons/case-writer.md); when a lesson there is confirmed (twice, or once by the producer, who is a native speaker), the Slovenian editor adds it here as a rule. **✔ native** marks a rule a native speaker confirmed; the rest are the studio's best judgement.

A future language gets its own file next to this one (`it.md`, `es.md` …) with the same sections.

## 1. Reading level (level A, children 7–8)
- **≤ 8 words a line**: sentences, call, film captions, hints, reveal, excuse, think, letter, puzzle texts. »…«, »No ja …« and emoji count as words. A sentence in `sentences` counts whole, quotes included.
- The checker doesn't count reveal, excuse, letter and `card.think` in step cases, and counts lock hints as one line: count those by hand.
- One idea per sentence. Concrete nouns a child can picture. Names, not pronouns.
- Avoid a negation that flips a clue (a child often misses »ne«), unless the season's knob asks for it.
- Prefer common, short words; avoid stacked consonants and long compounds (»pustolovščina« is hard on a tag).
- Call lines ≤ about 35 characters; scene labels one short word, never a function word (»kot«).

## 2. Reading knobs in Slovenian (the examples for CURRICULUM.md)
| Knob | Level A now | Later |
|---|---|---|
| Time words | najprej, potem, na koncu, zjutraj, zvečer, ob osmih | preden, medtem ko, šele ko, od … do |
| Pronouns | names only | »je rekla«, »njegov« pointing to the sentence before |
| Negation | rare | »ni«, »nikoli«, »nihče« |

## 3. Grammar traps
- **The dual** for two of anything, even without »dva«: »Žlička in žica sta skriti«, »Rezultata je povedal Lan«.
- **Numbers:** 2 → dual (dva dneva), 3–4 → nominative plural (trije / štirje dnevi), 5+ → genitive (pet dni). »Še štiri dni« is a slip. ✔ native
- **Case after prepositions:** v + locative (place) / accusative (motion), pri, za, pod, okoli + genitive.
- **Past-tense gender** (je bil / je bila), and **aspect** (je odprl ≠ je odpiral).
- **Clitics** never open a sentence: »Ali ga odkriješ?«, not »Ga odkriješ?«; order »se mi je«, »ga je«.
- **A time word with the present tense** reads as a habit: next to past-tense lines keep the past (»Opoldne so bili listi …«).
- **Two names side by side** read as one full name: put the verb between them (»Tim bral Maji pravljico«).
- **Impersonal sound verbs** work (»je piskalo«, »je ropotalo«), action verbs don't: »je nekdo tipkal«, not »je tipkalo«.
- Standard forms in child text, not colloquial: »ni prava« (not »ne paše«), »s teboj« (not »s tabo«).

## 4. Names
- Names are bold in every form, so every form must match the character's pattern `re` in `CHARACTERS`: cases and possessives (Tima, Timov, Elina, Kikin).
- Feminine names in -i stay undeclined (z Rozi, ujela Gusti); the possessive is short: **Rozin listek**, not Rozijin. ✔ native
- `caught` is the accusative, read inside »Detektivka Ana je ujela ___!« (Tima, Elo, Kiki).
- Kin words can match another character (»dedkov«): write »dedek Ivo«.

## 5. Who says ti and who says Vi
- The game says **ti** to the child.
- The child says **Vi** to old people and to staff: gospa Vera, the museum staff (Breda, Rambo, Ivo, Mila). Keep each adult's address the same across the whole season. The formal past participle stays masculine plural: »Kje ste bili?« ✔ native
- Parent-facing copy (landing page, adult screens) uses formal **vi**: »Povlecite«, »Igrajte skupaj«.

## 6. The child's gender
- The detective can be a boy or a girl. UI lines in the past tense that address the child have both forms with `ga(m, f)`: »Prav si ugibal / ugibala«, or a phrasing without gender.
- Case data can't switch gender: no »Hej, detektiv!« in a call or letter. Use »Hej, pomagaj!«, »Hvala!«.

## 7. Punctuation and voice
- Speech in »…« quotes. An ellipsis is »…« (one character).
- Instruction cards: one action per sentence, the goal question first, then the action, pointing at an icon the child can see (»Tapni 💬«).
- Watch calques from English: »velike črke« (not »veliki stavki«), »sklep« (not »zaključek«).
- The same thing has the same name across a season (»zračnik« everywhere).

## 8. Language-bound devices (flag them for other markets)
Clues that work only because of Slovenian wording must be marked **language-bound** in the season plan, so another language rebuilds them instead of translating:
- **Gender in the past tense gives the culprit away:** »je vzela« already says a woman or girl did it. The question uses the generic »Kdo je vzel …?« (as in every case so far), and no sentence describes the unnamed doer in the culprit's gender before the proof: use »Nekdo je …« or a trace instead. The fair-play tester checks it.
- Word puzzles (word search, cipher, torn note, a geslo) are built from Slovenian letters: Č, Š, Ž are one cell; no rude word by chance in any line.
- Puns and sound jokes.
