# Content strategy

The editorial director's plan of record: who the game is for, what success means, and what comes next. Read before every season brief. Keep it under 80 lines. The producer approves every change of direction.

## The audience
- Slovenian children learning to read. **The focus is 7–8: nail it for them.** 9–10 follows only if those children show interest; under 7 is out of scope for now (the landing page still says 6–10). Played on a parent's phone or iPad.
- **A child of 7–8 must be able to play alone.** They differ a lot in how much help they need, so the text stays the same and the game's help changes per child (CURRICULUM.md, »Help that fades«; producer, 2026-11-12).
- The parent is the second audience: they choose the game, may read along, and decide whether the child plays again.

## The goal
**The most fun detective game in the world for children learning to read**: Agatha Christie for 7-year-olds. The child reads to win, and wants to read the next case.

## What success looks like (from the playtests and the debrief)
- The child finishes a season and asks for the next one.
- Most cases are solved with 0–1 hints, few assisted; the child reads on their own.
- The child laughs, repeats a line, or talks about the story away from the screen (PLAYTEST.md).
- The parent says the child read more than usual.

**Cadence:** one new season a week (RELEASES.md), pushed before its day with `opens`.

## What we know works (evidence)
- A big mystery made of small oddities, with a red thread across the cases (👾, PLAYTEST 2026-11-01).
- The number lock 🔐 and numbers in general: one in every season (PLAYTEST 2026-11-05).
- Children skip instructions: every step opens with its instruction card (PLAYTEST 2026-11-02).

## Open questions for the next playtests
- Is the weekly cadence right for the child, or does it outrun them (do they finish a season before the next opens)?
- Is level A still right after four seasons, or is the child ready for 9–10 words a sentence?
- Which children of 7–8 need a parent, and for what: the rules, a hard word, finding the sentence, or staying with it? This shapes the warm-up cases and the fading help.
- Do 9–10-year-olds enjoy the seasons at level A, or are they bored?
- Which feeling did the child talk about most: the phone call, the unmasking, the lock, the newspaper?

## Next seasons (plan of 2026-11-17; the producer's direction: holidays bring more testers, so the theme must thrill 7–8-year-olds and pull them back to older cases)
**Evidence is thin:** PLAYTEST.md has nothing since 2026-11-02 (no muzej, pokal or sneg notes, no debrief numbers). The plan therefore moves one rung a week only on paper: after every playtest the director may hold a rung, swap neighbours or insert a consolidation season (12-10 is one already).

**Holiday rules.** (1) A new tester plays the *newest* season first, so the reading load is frozen at level A (≤ 8 words) from 12-03 on; the holidays grow the skill and the feeling, not the reading. (2) Every season stands alone; the thread below is a bonus, never needed. (3) Miklavž, Božiček and Dedek Mraz are never unmasked as someone's parent; a costumed helper is clearly a helper (producer to confirm). (4) People, not animals, as culprits; the gift ending (muzej, pokal) returns only on 12-24, where it belongs.

**The red thread: Ajda's challenge.** Ajda (sneg's rival, her return left open) sends a postcard at the end of every season from 11-26: one riddle about an earlier season (only a child who played it can answer, so a new tester goes back) and one object for her »kovček skrivnosti« (Bip's spoon, the golden ball, Strela's year, her ice magnifier). She appears in person in 12-17 or 12-10 and at the New Year duel on 12-31, where the collected objects help but are never required. Optional small build (producer decides, developer): the ID card shows one slot per season, filled when a season is finished; empty slots are the pull. Fallback: letters only.

**Calendar** (A = pitch day; push with `opens` two days before): sitting A 11-17, 11-24, 12-01, 12-08, then **12-10 for 12-24 and 12-15 for 12-31**, so both holiday seasons are pushed by 12-18 and 12-21 and the producer's holidays are free. Dates in [RELEASES.md](RELEASES.md).

**January:** if debriefs say level A is easy, level B (9–10 words) starts then, never in the holidays. **Stop:** one theme family for six weeks tires children; each December season changes place and kind of fun (home, stage, tent) even when the mood is Christmas.

## Markets
- **Now: Slovenian only.** All effort goes into making the Slovenian seasons as good as they can be, and validating them with real children.
- **Later: more languages** (Italian, Spanish, maybe English). Not before the Slovenian game is validated. The studio stays ready for it at no extra cost (docs/studio/README.md, »Ready for more languages«): language-neutral season skeletons, one style guide per language, language-bound clues flagged.
